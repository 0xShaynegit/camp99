## Background reference: "End of the Spear" / Waodani story (context for Bob's bio)

The Waorani/Waodani/Huarani/Waos are the indigenous people of the Amazonian region of Ecuador; "Waorani" means "true people"/"humans" in their own language. Historically fierce resistance to outside contact; semi-nomadic hunter-gatherers deeply tied to the forest, sometimes described as a Stone-Age existence. Ancestral lands threatened historically by oil exploration and illegal logging, and by conflict with both indigenous rivals and settlers.

Within the Wao, violent tribal groups were called "Auca" (a pejorative meaning roughly "savage"), who lived by pre-emptive killing logic tied to inter-family vendettas (spear, machete). This worsened when Shell arrived for oil — the Auca saw Shell workers as a threat and began killing them; Shell workers retaliated with firearms. Tribe numbers dwindled toward extinction.

Missionaries Ed McCully, Jim Elliot, Nate Saint, Pete Fleming, and Roger Youderian (ages 27-32) began systematic contact — air-dropping gifts (machetes, axes, pots, ribbons, clothing, and pets, which were reciprocated) — hoping to reverse the group's decline. Due to the culture of violence, the five men were speared to death within hours of face-to-face contact. Their deaths made national headlines and were the cover story of the Jan. 30, 1956 Time magazine issue.

Two widows of the martyred missionaries returned to the Auca and continued the work; the tribe's culture of violence ended. Mincayani, the man responsible for spearing Nate Saint, later became a surrogate father to Nate's son Steve Saint; their father/son relationship lasted until Mincayani's death (he was among the tribe's first grandfathers — men before the Auca's conversion to Christianity rarely lived long enough to become grandfathers).

A movie was made for the 50th anniversary of the event. Ecuador's jungles presented logistical problems, so Panama's jungles stood in, with members of the Emberá tribe representing the Waodani.

It was in Panama that photographer Robert Bowling (Bob) formed a strong connection with Marco Flaco, the 13-year-old cast to play young Mincayani. Marco's sister MiLizbeth Flaco and a brother also had parts in the film. Bob later brought Marco to the USA as an AFS Intercultural Programs exchange student, and Marco lived with Bob's family — adjusting from a home without electricity and a life of hunting/fishing for the community, while keeping his room, clothes, and shoes meticulously neat. Marco attended Shawnee Mission North for the 2008-2009 school year. (Related reference: Emberá Quera Panamá / Emberá Village Tours.)
